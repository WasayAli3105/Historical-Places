KAMCHATKA PENINSULA

The Kamchatka Peninsula, situated in the Russian Far East, is a rugged and remote landmass renowned for its stunning natural beauty and extraordinary biodiversity. Jutting out from the eastern edge of Siberia into the Pacific Ocean, Kamchatka is characterized by a dramatic landscape shaped by volcanic activity, tectonic forces, and extreme climate conditions. One of the most notable features of Kamchatka is its cluster of around 300 volcanoes, 29 of which are still active. This volcanic activity has created a dynamic environment of hot springs, geysers, and other geothermal phenomena, earning Kamchatka the nickname "Land of Fire and Ice." Among its most famous landmarks is the towering Klyuchevskaya Sopka, the highest active volcano in Eurasia, standing at over 4,750 meters (15,580 feet)...

PUTORANA PLATEAU

The Putorana Plateau, located in the remote reaches of northern Siberia, is a vast and virtually untouched wilderness of breathtaking beauty and ecological significance. Stretching over 250,000 square kilometers (about 97,000 square miles), this expansive plateau is characterized by rugged mountains, deep river valleys, and countless lakes, many of which are pristine and untouched by human activity. One of the most remarkable features of the Putorana Plateau is its geological diversity, with ancient volcanic formations, towering cliffs, and deep canyons carved by glaciers and rivers over millions of years. This rugged terrain is home to a wealth of plant and animal life, including rare and endemic species adapted to the harsh Arctic climate...
